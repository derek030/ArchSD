Our Strategy and Approach

We integrate sustainable development considerations into every stage of project delivery and facet of our operations. We have established a set of policies, guidelines and system controls, striving to maintain the highest standards across our services and operations:

Quality, Environmental, Anti-bribery, Occupational Health and Safety Policy

Our commitment to long-term sustainability is reflected in the Quality, Environmental, Anti-bribery, Occupational Health and Safety Policy and is communicated to all governance bodies, employees and business partners including contractors. The Department upholds high professional standards in service delivery while promoting environmentally responsible practices, including energy conservation, pollution prevention and resource efficiency. It is committed to ensuring a safe and healthy working environment by managing risks. ArchSD also ensures compliance with applicable legislation while striving to exceed statutory requirements, supported by adequate resources and training. In addition, the Department actively promotes these principles across the construction industry and maintains a zero-tolerance approach to bribery. For full policy, please refer to Appendix.

Integrated Management System

To align our services and operations with globally recognised benchmarks, the ArchSD has mplemented a robust Integrated Management System (IMS) comprising aspects of quality, environmental, anti-bribery, occupational health and safety and energy management for a unified framework for operational excellence. We also refer to the ISO 26000:2010 for guidance on operating in a socially responsible way. To foster continuous improvements, we conduct annual performance reviews of the IMS to ensure ongoing compliance and improvement. Certifications complied include the IMS, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, ISO 37001:2016, ISO 45001:2018, and ISO 50001:2018 Accredited Certifications.

Supporting the UNSDGs

Renewable Infrastructure integration

Incorporating thin-film solar photovoltaics across government properties, driving localized clean energy grids directly into the community fabric.

Lifecycle Material Management

Prioritizing modular architectural designs and high-recycled-content materials, ensuring minimal waste footprint during decommissioning phases.

Biophilic Community Districts

Blending urban green parks directly with new structure pathways to combat urban heat island configurations and improve public physical wellness.
